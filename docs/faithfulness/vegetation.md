## Tree crowns, species silhouettes, bare planes (2026-11-03, round 3 follow-up)

Round 3 ranked the trees as the most noticeable problem left: crowns were flat, identical plates ("pads of diamonds"), the stone pine, holm oak, ficus and winter plane did not read as themselves, the palms were leaf plates, and every plane had the same three-limb Y trunk. All of it is in `src/treegeo.js` (rewritten), with small call-site changes in `src/props.js`, `src/species.js` and `src/world.js`. Tree positions and the random stream of the tile builders are unchanged (the variant and bare choices below come from position hashes, not from the tile's generator).

**How a crown is built now.** A crown is a few lobes (ellipsoids) that sit on the tips of real branches. Over the shell of every lobe lie small open leaf sprays (`Mini.sprig`: a fan of 5 triangles to a ragged ring that alternates long and short points, cupped so the edges hang, 0.5 to 1.4 m across) or, for pines, tufts of needles; a lobe also holds a solid octahedron core a little darker than the leaves, so the crown is a mass and not a hollow shell. A spray is skipped where it would lie inside another lobe. The lighting normal of a spray is mostly the normal of the whole crown (so the crown shades as one thing, light on top, dark below and in the middle) and only partly its own face, which is what turns the old diamonds into a continuous canopy with a ragged edge. Trunks and limbs are tubes along bent polylines (a seeded branching generator, `boughs`) with mottled bark, not straight frustums, so the three-limb Y of the old planes, which looked like a cartoon, is gone: a plane now forks into four or five limbs at once, each bending and tapering, and the crown starts lower so most of the fork is hidden. The material is two-sided (sprays are open sheets): the `dbl` props material, and `treeMat` in `world.js`.

**Species.**
- Plane: leafy (two individuals, `plane`, `plane_b`: different trunk lean, limb count and crown) and bare (`plane_bare`, `plane_bare_b`: the same skeleton with no leaves: four primary limbs, three forks on each, and a broom of twigs on every end). The bare ones are used where the photos are of a bare tree: Via Ernesto Basile (su13, March), Viale Michelangelo (zi08, zi09, March) by street name in the `CANOPY` table of `props.js`, and the planes within 140 m of Piazza Indipendenza (ce12, February; no plane happens to stand there, so this changes nothing today). Everywhere else the planes are leafy (Libertà and Ruggero Settimo are June and September in the photos). The photos are of every season and the game has one, so which avenue is bare is a choice per photo; it is not a seasonal system. Pollarded planes (the knuckled limbs of Basile in the photo) are not modelled, the bare plane stands for them.
- Stone pine (`pine` in the props, `stone` in the woods): a straight bare trunk to 8 m, dark red-brown, four stout limbs, and a wide flat umbrella of dark blue-green cloud pads. Second individual `pine_b` (leaning trunk, other pads). The wood versions (`stone_lo`) keep a short trunk and 3 pads.
- Holm oak (new model `holm`, used by the species `holm` of `species.js`, which was the generic broadleaf with a tint): a short thick trunk, a dense very dark lobed dome. The woods' road trees and `holm_lo` use the same shape.
- Ficus: a very thick fluted trunk, five heavy limbs spread wide, a very wide, low, dark dome (crown about 14 m across before the instance scale).
- Palms: Washingtonia (`palm_fan_a/b/c`) is a slender grey trunk in rings of leaf scars with a dead skirt and a head of 22 to 28 palmate fronds, each a stalk with nine narrow blades that part towards the tips, young ones standing up and old ones hanging; Phoenix (`palm_date_a/b`) is a thick pale trunk with a swelling and 24 to 25 arching feather fronds (a midrib with a comb of leaflet triangles on both sides), the young ones upright.
- Aleppo pine and the generic broadleaf and the orange tree use the same spray crowns (small, light, airy for the Aleppo).

**Variety.** Two kinds that cost nothing at run time: every tree instance now gets its own aspect (width and height scale independently, 0.86 to 1.14 and 0.82 to 1.18) and a small hue and brightness shift, both from its position (`TREE_LIKE` in `props.js`, `instantiate`); and the crowns themselves are lopsided, so the random turn about the vertical already gives a different outline. One kind that costs a draw call per cell and model: the second individual of the planes, the bare planes and the stone pines (`TREE_VARIANT`, chosen by a position hash in `placeTree`, half and half).

## Trees: solid crowns, buttressed ficus, lighter palms, stone-pine rows (2026-11-04, round 4 gap 1)

Round 4 ranked the trees first among the visible gaps: flat plates on identical trunks, no stone pines along Viale Regione Siciliana (su08, su09), oleander as green balls with pink lumps. This pass rebuilt the tree shapes of `src/treegeo.js`, changed a few tree lines of `src/props.js`, `src/species.js` and `src/world.js`, and added no alpha-tested leaves (nothing is discarded in the fragment shader; every leaf is geometry).

**How a crown is built now.** The round 3 crowns were hollow shells of small sprays (5 triangles each), which read as shards and cost 400 to 600 triangles. A crown is now a few solid lobes (`Mini.lobe`: a jittered icosahedron of 20 or 80 faces, stretched, flattened underneath, light on top and dark under and inside, lit with the normal of the whole crown) on the tips of real branches, plus a fringe of 1 to 3 sprays per lobe to break the outline. A lobe lies where the real crown has a clump; faces inside another lobe are not drawn. The same lobes make the cheap `*_lo` pines and oaks of the woods. Limbs get fewer segments on the finer levels (`nseg` can now be a list per level).

**Smells met on the way.**
- A model needs six hand-kept tables in `props.js` (`MODELS`, `SPECS`, `TREE_LIKE`, `SCALE_R`, `TALL`, `FAR`) plus the species code in `species.js`; a variant `_b` needs all of them again. One registration function would remove most of the edits and the chance of forgetting one.
- `baseMatrix` (`props.js`) composes a reset tree with a uniform scale, so a tree that was knocked over and put back after 25 s loses the width and height variety it was built with (`instantiate` gives it a different aspect per tree).
- The `crown` field of `SPECS` is a size hint for lidar-measured trees (`it.r / crown`) and does not equal the crown radius of the model (the planes were always about a third larger than their `crown`); I kept the ratio of old to new, so lidar trees keep their size.
- `palm_fan_c` is registered and has a collision entry but nothing picks it (`placeTree` callers use `palm_fan_a` and `palm_fan_b`).
- `world.js` `buildTrees` keeps one list per kind by hand (`rows`, `mats`, `smats`, `hmats`); a map from kind to list would make the third individual of a wood tree a one-line change.
- The road rows of the woods cap at `MAX = 1400` trees per tile, counted before the shape is known; a dense tile stops planting mid-road.
- Streaming is not deterministic: two runs of the same build at the Favorita saw 3,259 and 4,011 tree instances (the number of woods cells in view when the streamer went quiet), so compare triangles per tree there, not the sum.
