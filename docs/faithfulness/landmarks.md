Method: real references (Wikimedia Commons, Wikipedia, Google Maps satellite for looking only, never copied into the repo), then the same viewpoint in the real game with headless Chrome, list differences, fix, re-shoot.

## Stadio Renzo Barbera (priority, rebuilt)

Compared: Google Maps satellite (oblique), Commons photos (aerial plan, Via del Fante street front, interior, curve exterior), the seating plan, OSM footprint (outer ring 215 x 149 m, playing-area ring 144 x 78 m, long axis SSE).
What the real stadium is: an elongated oval with straight sides and round ends, two tiers split by a rose-coloured walkway, teal-green and blue plastic seats, ONE roofed stand (the Tribuna on the west-south-west long side, a deep pale cantilever roof with a teal fascia and white pylons), the other long side and both ends open to the sky, pale concrete outside with a pylon per bay, a blue-teal window band and a teal eave, tall white floodlight masts at the corners, a metal fence with sponsor boards round the plaza.
What was wrong before: teal canopies on BOTH long sides, saturated green seats, grey facade with a roman-amphitheatre look, no roof over the real main stand, no fence, the red running track hidden by the tile's raised ground layer (the pitch mesh was 3 cm above it; now 0.8 m above the terrain with polygon offset, the red track stays as requested).
Now: 120-sample bowl from the OSM rings, lower tier to ~9 m, rose walkway, upper tier to ~21 m, radial gangways, concrete facade (pylons, windows, teal band), teal eave ring, Tribuna roof (top pale, underside dark grey, tall teal back fascia, pylons), fence 13 m out with sponsor boards, four 52 m masts, the vehicle gate (see Drive-in), 7.8k triangles.
Before/after (same six viewpoints): /private/tmp/claude-501/lm/stad/before/b1..b6.png and /private/tmp/claude-501/lm/stad/after/b1..b6.png.
Drive-in (board item e5): the vehicle gate is the main entrance in the middle of the front, the covered Tribuna on the long side towards Viale del Fante (the OSM roof polygon is that stand, Via Cassarà and the OSM service road end square on the middle of its wall); an entrance block with the name and two arched side doors stands out of the wall, the tunnel runs through the lower tier onto the track (src/lmc-a.js, GATE), the fences and bollards across the access road are left out (clearFences, src/tree-clear.js); the old gate at the north end is closed stands and facade again. The playing area is now a flat platform (src/stadium-ground.js: inside the OSM playing-area ring the ground is a flat 48.0 m, reached over a 6 m ramp from the ring's edge, applied to hooks.ground in src/world.js, and it counts as raised ground so the lapa stands on the track like on a pavement; the terrain mesh under it is flat at 47.8 m), the red track and the pitch are drawn on it (src/lmc-a.js), the stands (ring walls) and both goals (box over the mouth and the net) are solid. The stands hold a crowd of rosanero fans (src/stadium-crowd.js: one InstancedMesh, about half the seats, LPC fans of the cast seen from the front, tools/lpc/tifosi.mjs makes assets/peds/lpc/tifosi.png), animated in the vertex shader: a wave of raised scarves goes round the bowl every 16 s, and while the lapa is on the pitch (src/stadium.js) everybody jumps and now and then a chant balloon appears.
Not done: the diagonal stair ramps on the exterior, the roofs of the curved ends (stepped teal bays), car-park markings (ground layers belong to the land cover work).

## Engineering notes
- `crag()` in lmc-kit.js: jittered frustum for cliff footings.
- Models place everything relative to `groundRange` of the footprint; new models use `front(key)` (box axis facing the nearest road) and repeating facade textures with v aligned to the base (`body()` in lmc-d.js).
- Landmark `skipIds` hide only the generic mesh; collision walls of the generic footprints stay (so models stay inside their footprints).
- Seen in the logs, not mine: `src/sea.js` ring shader uses the reserved word `patch` as a variable name (GLSL error "Illegal use of reserved word" at line ~163 of the fragment shader, appears in strict-validation Chrome runs).

## The centro landmarks read the streamed ground (board item x6)

The ten centro models in `src/landmarks.js` (Quattro Canti, Fontana Pretoria, Palazzo delle Aquile, Teatro Massimo, Cattedrale, Porta Nuova, Porta Felice, San Cataldo, Santa Caterina, Palazzo dei Normanni) used to be built once at start-up, when `groundY` still answered from the old baked centro grid. They are now `CENTRO_LANDMARKS` (name, x, z, radius, build), built by `src/world.js` like the city landmarks: once the tile they stand on has terrain, and the tiles their footprint reaches into have too (or are not coming; after 8 s it builds anyway). The group is kept as the part `lm:<name>` of that tile, so it survives the tile's detail changes (the distant skyline keeps its landmarks) and is built again after the tile was released. `main.js` no longer builds them; `prepareLandmarks` still marks the generic buildings they replace. `?nolm=1` still skips them. Build time on the dev machine: 1 to 32 ms each (Quattro Canti the longest), 87 ms for all.

Height of the old baked grid against the streamed tiles at the model's anchor (m, baked minus streamed): Normanni +9.2 (the tower stood 9 m too high), Porta Nuova +6.0 (hence the 35.5 constant, now removed: the base is `groundY` at the passage), Cattedrale +1.2, Santa Caterina -1.1, Pretoria -1.8, the rest within 0.5.

Footing: every model's lowest vertices against the streamed ground under them (`groundcheck.mjs`, median / 5th / 95th percentile, m): Quattro Canti +0.1 / -0.3 / +0.5, Pretoria +0.0 / -0.4 / +0.7 (was +1.2: the lidar reads the basin itself as ground, so the base is now the level of the piazza 12 m round it and a skirt hangs from the fence ring), Porta Felice base is the lowest ground under the 11 x 10.6 m pylon box and a skirt hangs round it (the +1.0 the check reports there is the two fountain basins, which stand 0.9 m up on their stems), Teatro -0.2, Cattedrale 0.0, Porta Nuova -0.1, Aquile -1.4, San Cataldo -1.0, Santa Caterina -1.9, Normanni -0.7. Negative is buried on the high side of a slope, which is fine; nothing floats more than the basins and plinths that are meant to stand off the ground.

## Quattro Canti rebuilt (board item c10)

References: Wikimedia Commons, "Palermo-Quattro-Canti-bjs2007-01.jpg" (CC0, front view of one facade), "Quattro Canti, Palermo, Sicily, Italy (9455477141).jpg" (Flickr, CC BY 2.0, two facades from the piazza); viewed for proportions only, nothing of them is stored. Real facade as read from them: a rusticated panelled pedestal, three tiers between two grey marble monolithic columns each (Doric, Ionic, composite pilasters), a deep curved balcony on corbels with an iron railing between the first two tiers, in the lowest tier a three-stage fountain (lower basin with a garland, upper basin, the season in marble) and a white inscription cartouche, in the middle tier a dark shell-headed niche with the Spanish king and green-shuttered windows with pediments and angels, in the top tier the patron saint's niche with an angel on a cloud over it, small windows in white stucco frames, an attic with end volutes and shields and the royal arms (eagle and crowned shield) on top, two black three-arm candelabra in front of the side bays, honey-coloured weathered limestone, white statues and stucco, green shutters.
Model (`paintQC`, `buildQuattroCanti`): the four concave arcs are found as before; each facade is a faceted concave wall painted on one 768 x 1536 texture (blocks that get finer with height, the panelled pedestal, quoin strips, stucco window frames with shutters, cornice shadows, metope band, attic panels, streaks and a darkened foot) with the heavy parts as geometry: arc slabs for the pedestal and the three entablatures with modillions, the corbelled balcony, six columns with their capitals, the garlanded fountain with water, the season and the king and the saint on pedestals in shell-headed niches, window pediments with angels, the crown with arms, two lamps, and a skirt where the ground falls away. ## Collision for Fontana Pretoria, the Teatro Massimo stairs and the Cathedral porch (board item x4)

The centro landmark descriptors (`CENTRO_LANDMARKS`) now carry `walls` ([x0, z0, x1, z1] segments, built at module load from the same anchors the models use, `COLLISION` in `src/landmarks.js`). `registerPhysics` in `src/world.js` adds them to the wall grid with the anchor tile while it is at full detail, exactly as it does the building footprints; the lapa, the pedestrians and the traffic all treat them as walls. No `dropWalls` were needed.

| landmark | what is solid | what is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fontana Pretoria | the iron fence ring (r 12.05 m round the fountain's centre, 32 segments) and the basin wall inside it (r 10.45 m, 24 segments) | nothing inside is reachable |
| Teatro Massimo | one block of 28 x 25 m: the portico podium, the 24 steps and the cheek walls (from the portico's back plane 14.5 m to 39.6 m in front of the dome axis), plus a 3.2 x 3.6 m plinth for each of the two bronze lions at the foot of the stairs | the piazza up to the foot of the stairs |
| Cattedrale | the south porch: a 23.8 x 5.7 m rectangle (the block behind the three arches, the screen in front, the two pylons) | the rest of the cathedral is its OSM footprint, which already had walls |

Decision on the Massimo stairs: the lapa does not drive up them. The model has 24 steps of about 15 cm rise and 53 cm going (3.6 m up over 12.7 m, a 16 degree stair, not a ramp), the real ones are for people, and a vehicle that could climb a 15 cm kerb 24 times would be a different vehicle. So the stairs are a wall and the lapa stops at the bottom step (drive test: 9 m/s straight at the stairs, it stops 1.5 m short of the front edge of the block, which is the lapa's own radius). A drivable ramp would need a level surface under it (`hooks.level`, as the bridges use) and was not worth it. Pedestrians also route round the block now; they used to walk over the stairs model.
Drive tests (`drive.mjs` in the report folder, 9 m/s, the lapa forced at its start speed): the Cathedral porch stops it 2.9 m from the porch's origin, which is its front face plus the lapa's radius; the Pretoria fence stops it at 13.5 m from the centre (12.05 + the lapa's radius) from two directions (the other directions start inside other walls in the piazza).

## Cattedrale: dome, towers, interlaced arcades (board item c11)

References (proportions only, nothing is stored): Commons "Palermo Cathedral with Clock Tower.jpg" (CC0), "Cattedrale PA 01.JPG" and "Palermo Duomo 03.jpg" (CC BY-SA 4.0). What they show: the dome (Fuga, 1801) is tall and ovoid on a round drum with paired pilasters, round-headed windows and a cornice with a pinnacle over every pilaster, ribbed in grey-blue lead, with a lantern, a small cupola and a cross; the clock tower is a slim campanile with corner colonnettes, a big white clock under a bracket cornice with the inscription OPERIBVS CREDITE, a belfry with twin pointed openings under a relieving arch, corner turrets, gables and an octagonal spire; the walls carry Arab-Norman interlaced (intersecting) pointed arches in dark lava and pale tuff under the battlements, over pointed blind arcades.
Model (`buildCattedrale`): the drum is a 16-sided cylinder with a painted tile of pilaster pair, window and dentilled cornice, 6 m high, with a ring of 16 pinnacles; the dome is a lathe with an ovoid profile (about 8.8 m high on a 9.3 m radius) and a painted gore with rib and lead rolls, repeated 16 times; the lantern is 3 m of eight arched openings with a cupola and a cross. The four towers were rebuilt: painted shaft, corner colonnettes, string course, a belfry stage with its own painted twin-arch face, turrets with cones, a gable on each face, an octagonal lead spire; the south-west one is 34 m (was 31) and carries the clock (a 3.3 m painted disc with numerals and hands, and the inscription plate under it). The interlaced arcade is painted into the wall bay texture (two rows of arches half a period apart with crossing strokes and lava dots). Triangles: 9,570 (was 7,400). Textures added: drum 256 x 416, dome 64 x 256, belfry 320 x 384, clock 256 x 256, inscription 384 x 48.

## La Cuba, San Giovanni degli Eremiti, the Palazzo dei Normanni front, the Stazione Marittima (board item c4, `src/lmc-e.js`)

The four models moved out of `src/lmc-b.js` and `src/lmc-c.js` into `src/lmc-e.js` (the old builders are gone; `landmarks-city.js` imports them from there). Same method as the centro landmarks: painted textures for the walls, real geometry for what stands out of them, one bin per material. They stand on the streamed ground: city landmarks are built in `buildFull` after the tile's streets and trees, and `src/world.js` now waits (8 s at most, `lmNeighboursPending`) for the tiles round the model that are on their way to terrain, so a model near a tile border (the Normanni footprint reaches 100 m into the next tile) reads the same heights the terrain mesh uses. Every footing is the footprint's highest ground plus 0.1 m with a stone foundation skirt down to the lowest ground under it; measured against `groundY`, the floor line of the models is +0.1 to +0.2 m and nothing floats.

## Quattro Canti and Piazza Pretoria, round 5 (survey round 4, gap 4)

Round 4: the Quattro Canti have the concave fronts, columns and niches but no carved stone, statues or balcony depth.

Quattro Canti (`carveQC`, `statueFine`, `src/landmarks.js`): carving painted on the wall texture, which is the same size as before (768 x 1536, one per model), so no geometry and no canvas is added: a scrolled cartouche (volutes, a scallop over it) round the plaque, whose bars are now the lines of an inscription (the words are an estimate, the real text is not read from the photographs), a rosette in every metope of the Doric frieze, egg-and-dart on the three cornices, the modillions under them, mascarons on the keystones of the two lower windows with volutes beside them, husk chains down their frames, a lozenge and rosette in each pedestal panel, soft shade beside the columns and a deep shadow under the balcony slab, golden and brown patina clouds. The modillions were 54 boxes a facade; they are painted now, which pays for the rest. The three figures per facade are `statueFine`: a neck, a bigger head, one arm up, a crown and sceptre on the king and the season, a halo on the saint (about 60 more triangles each than `statue`).

Drawn from Mapillary frames of the crossing (2024, six headings from the centre, looked at, nothing copied or stored) for what is carved where, and from the Commons photos named above for the proportions. The faces, the inscription and the figures' attributes are an estimate.

Cost, counted from the loaded meshes at the model (30 m wide): marble 19568 to 19936 triangles, ashlar 9136 to 6544, flat unchanged: 2224 triangles fewer, the same meshes and draw calls.

Piazza Pretoria (`src/fences.js`, `src/props.js`, `src/landmarks.js`): the fountain was hidden, not plain. OSM maps its precinct as two walls and the bake gives a wall a plausible 2.5 m, so from a piazza eye the whole fountain stood behind a sand-coloured slab (hpr_S, hpr_E, hpr_N, hpr_W), and the thirteen OSM statues of the precinct were drawn as the 4 m obelisk of `monument`. Now: those two walls are drawn 0.45 m high (`vh`, `lowPrecinct`) and keep their 2.5 m for how they break, collide and fly apart; the statues of the precinct are `monument_s`, a figure on a plinth (the one in the middle of the basin is not drawn, it is in the model behind a fence); the model has gadroons (ten lobes, a scallop at the head of each) painted in a band on its three basins, and the 42 ring figures are `statueLite` (about 80 triangles, a third of `statue`). The ring figures and the iron fence were already there; they were behind the wall.

Drawn from the Mapillary frames of the piazza (2021 and 2024, five headings, looked at, nothing copied or stored): the real precinct is a low plinth under a black iron railing, the basins are carved in lobes, the figures stand on the rims. The gadroon count and the figures' shape are an estimate.

Cost, counted from the loaded meshes at the model: marble 11432 triangles became `pret` 6980 (4452 fewer), the same three meshes (pret, dark, rail), so the same draw calls; one more 256 x 256 canvas (`pret`, painted once per session like the others) replaces the use of `marble` in this model; the wall quads are the same number; the monument figure is smaller than the obelisk it replaces.
